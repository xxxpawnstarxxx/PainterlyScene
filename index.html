Curator’s notes

On a world that opened all at once

There is no one in this picture, and nothing you could hold. It is drawn from two feelings. The first is the afternoon a life changes on a single audacious idea — the strange, floating invincibility of it, the loud joy that follows, and the long, golden drive home through a warm night when every road seems to have been unlocked just for you. The second is older: a birthday, a vast new world discovered for the first time, hours vanishing in its glow, wrapped in warmth, and the messy, impatient ache to dive back in.

So the sun sits inside a colossal stone ring, a gate standing open. Islands hang in the air because they have simply declined to fall. A river of molten gold winds all the way home into the light, carrying streaks of momentum. Above the valley, chalk bursts of celebration flower and crumble. And in the hollow under the great fern — whose fiddlehead is still curled like a secret — a nest of warm seed pods trembles, hops and bursts, unable to wait one more second.

Every mark on the sheet was made by a simulated hand: a wrist that arcs, a tremor, a pressure that rises and lifts. Pigment is pressed through a physical model of the paper’s tooth, loads it as layers build, crumbles, skips and is dragged by the stump. Living elements are redrawn by that hand twelve times a second, and the contours boil between three separately searched drawings — the way a hand-drawn film breathes.
